Usage-Based Billing

Tokens, Credits, and USD

A simple guide to how AI usage is counted, priced, and shown on a bill.

01 — The Basics

What is a token?

Token

A small piece of text

AI does not count text exactly like people do. It breaks your words into small pieces called tokens.

Input

What you give the AI

Your prompt, instructions, and any added reference material all count as input.

Output

What the AI gives back

The answer from the AI is output. Input and output may have different prices.

Simple example: a short question costs less than a long document review, because there is less text for the AI to read and write.
02 — Usage Measurement

How tokens turn into credits

Tokens used → cost calculated

The platform counts how much text was processed, then applies the model’s price.

Cost → shown as credits

Some platforms show spending as credits instead of dollars.

Credits → deducted

As you use the service, credits are removed from your available balance.

Think of credits like arcade tokens: they make spending easier to track, but they still represent real money behind the scenes.
03 — Billing Conversion

How credits connect to dollars

Dollars are the real cost

The provider sets a price, often based on tokens or groups of tokens.

Credits represent that cost

A credit may equal a penny, a dollar, or another amount depending on the product.

Every platform can differ

The credit-to-dollar rate depends on the provider, plan, and model.

Simple example: tokens are like electricity usage. Credits are like the meter reading, and dollars are the final bill.
04 — Simple Formula

The simple billing flow

Start here when estimating cost

Most usage-based AI bills follow this path:

Tokens used × price = USD cost → credits deducted
This is the simple version. Some bills also include separate charges for input, output, tools, images, or other features.
05 — Key Takeaway

Tokens are the meter

Tokens measure how much work the AI does with text. Credits help show or track that usage. Dollars are the real cost behind it. To estimate cost, start with tokens, apply the model price, then convert that amount into credits or dollars.
